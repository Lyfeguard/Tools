How was this calculated?Method, assumptions and what to include

Net worth is calculated by subtracting your outstanding liabilities from your estimated asset values.

What counts as an asset?

Cash and savings, ISAs, other investments, pension pots, property, vehicles, business interests and other valuable assets. Use current estimates and a consistent approach to jointly owned assets and debts.

What counts as a liability?

Outstanding mortgage balances, credit cards, personal loans, car finance, student loans and other debts. Enter remaining balances, not monthly payments, and don't subtract debts twice. Student loan repayment obligations may differ from conventional borrowing.

Important to know

This is a gross balance-sheet estimate, not cash available today or an inheritance-tax valuation. State Pension benefits and defined-benefit pension income are not ordinary pension-pot balances. UK student loans generally have income-related repayments and may be written off under scheme rules. Ownership, leases, taxes, selling costs, valuations and currency changes may affect realisable wealth. An allocated share of a joint loan may not equal legal liability.
