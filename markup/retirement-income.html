    How was this calculated?

    Enter your details to see how the figures are worked out.

    Each year starts with taking the income your pot needs to provide: the income you want, less the State Pension (from State Pension age) and any other guaranteed income. What is left grows at your growth rate less inflation, so every figure stays in today’s money. The State Pension is assumed to rise with inflation. Income tax uses 2026/27 rates and allowances, and the Lump Sum Allowance of £268,275, kept at today’s values. Charges should already be taken off your growth rate.

    Worked example: £300,000 pension pot from age 60

    Taking £25,000 a year before tax from a £300,000 pension pot from age 60, with the full State Pension from 67, 4% growth and 2.5% inflation, the pot would last until age 79.

    To make it last to age 95, the income would need to be about £20,359 a year.

    Figures from HMRC Income Tax rates and the Lump Sum Allowance for 2026/27; the full new State Pension for 2026/27.
